The Donutful donut recall: a Class I warning in October, and a barcode that does not scan

FDAAllergensClass I Published October 10, 2026

On October 6, 2026, the FDA classified the recall of Donutful Chocolate Dipped Vanilla Cake Donuts as Class I, its most serious risk category, because the donuts may contain milk that is not listed on the label. The headlines are new; the recall is not. It began in August, the best-by date passed in September — and the barcode printed in the company's own recall notice cannot belong to any real product.

Which Donutful donuts are recalled

The recalling firm is The Better Bakehouse Snack Company of Las Vegas, Nevada. According to its notice and the FDA enforcement record (recall number H-0011-2027), the recall covers:

  • Donutful Chocolate Dipped Vanilla Cake Donuts, 10 mini donuts in a 7.05 oz carton containing five 1.41 oz (40 g) pouches of two donuts each;
  • Lot 26078, best if used by 9/15/2026;
  • UPC 850041392103 (see below why this matters);
  • 10,602 consumer units, made and distributed in March 2026, sold nationwide in stores and on Amazon.com.

The company's notice names no retailer other than Amazon.com. The problem surfaced after one allergic reaction was reported; the person has fully recovered. The firm says its contract manufacturer has identified the cause of the mislabeling and taken corrective measures.

Two official documents, two different barcodes

The company's recall notice, published by the FDA on August 24, gives the UPC as 3 50041 39210 3. The FDA's enforcement record gives it as 850041392103. Only one of them can be right, and the barcode itself says which.

The last digit of a 12-digit UPC is a check digit, calculated from the first eleven so that a scanner can catch a misread. Add the digits in odd positions and multiply by three, add the digits in even positions, and the check digit is whatever brings the total up to the next multiple of ten:

UPCCheck digit should beValid?
850041392103 (FDA record)3Yes
350041392103 (recall notice)8No

The number in the recall notice is very likely a one-digit typo — a 3 where an 8 belongs. It is a small error with a real cost: anyone who copies it into a search, a store system or a recall app is looking for a barcode that does not exist. If you are checking a carton, match 850041392103, and confirm with the lot number, 26078.

It is not the first time we have seen this. Earlier this month, the official records for a frozen berry recall wrote the same lot code three different ways. The identifiers a shopper needs are often the least carefully copied part of a recall.

Why the warning came two months later

The dates explain why a recall from the summer is in the news in October:

  • March 2026 — the donuts are made and distributed.
  • August 17 — recall initiated, according to the FDA record.
  • August 24 — the company's notice is published on the FDA website.
  • September 15 — the best-by date passes.
  • October 6 — the FDA classifies the recall as Class I.
  • October 14 — date of the FDA weekly enforcement report that carries it.

That sequence is normal. A company announces a recall first; the FDA reviews it and assigns a class weeks later. The classification measures how dangerous the problem is, not how new it is. Here, Class I means the agency judged there is a reasonable probability that eating the product could cause serious health consequences — which, for a milk allergy, is the point.

It also explains the word "massive" in some coverage. At 10,602 units, this is a modest recall by volume. What makes it serious is the class, not the size.

Why an expired snack still matters

Individually wrapped mini donuts are exactly the kind of food that outlives its best-by date: a pouch in a lunch box, a carton at the back of the pantry, a snack in a desk drawer or a sports bag. A best-by date is about quality, not safety, so many people eat past it. For a child with a milk allergy, a forgotten pouch from this lot is the risk, whatever the date says.

What to do

If anyone in your household has a milk allergy or severe sensitivity, look for Donutful Chocolate Dipped Vanilla Cake Donuts, lot 26078, best by 9/15/2026, UPC 850041392103. Do not eat them. Throw them away, or return them to the place of purchase for a refund with a receipt. Questions: The Better Bakehouse Snack Company, 800-243-0335, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. ET. Signs of a severe allergic reaction — trouble breathing, swelling of the throat or tongue, hives with dizziness — need emergency care: call 911.
