Kayem recall: six months of hot dogs, bacon and deli meats, and one warm cooler

USDAClass IReady-to-eat Published October 20, 2026

On October 17, 2026, the USDA's Food Safety and Inspection Service (FSIS) announced that Kayem Foods Inc., of Chelsea, Massachusetts, is recalling about 1.58 million pounds of ready-to-eat meat and poultry products. The products may have been stored at unsafe temperatures. FSIS rates the recall Class I, its highest risk level.

What happened

According to FSIS, the problem came to light on October 1, when a retail customer complained that products were warm on delivery. Kayem's investigation found that a warehouse cooler used to store various ready-to-eat products had experienced temperature deviations.

The recall reaches back much further than that complaint: it covers products made from about March 31 to October 7, 2026, more than six months of production. The items went to retail stores and food-service locations nationwide, and to Hong Kong.

FSIS reports no confirmed illnesses. Its notice does not name a specific bacterium. The concern with temperature abuse is that bacteria can multiply when food is not kept cold enough, and ready-to-eat products are, by definition, meant to be eaten without further cooking that would kill them.

Which products are affected

FSIS posts the full list as a PDF on its recall page (recall 026-2026). It covers a long range of ready-to-eat items, among them hot dogs and franks, bacon, ham, chicken sausage, bologna, corned beef, kielbasa and bratwurst. Product names on the list include the Kayem, al fresco, Genoa and Triple M brands.

Two details make this recall easier to check than most USDA recalls:

  • The establishment number. Every recalled item carries EST. M/P 7839, EST. P 20935A or EST. M 9520 inside the round USDA mark of inspection on the package.
  • Lot numbers and expiration dates. The FSIS product list gives, for each item, its SKU, UPC, lot number and expiration date. Many meat recalls only list a production date range, leaving shoppers to guess.

Because the list is long, check the package against it rather than relying on the product name alone: one brand can make both recalled and unaffected items.

Why the dates matter

Bacon, hot dogs and vacuum-packed sausages keep for weeks, and many end up in the freezer. A pack bought in the spring may still be at the back of the refrigerator or freezer today. Freezing does not make a recalled product safe: it pauses bacteria, it does not remove them.

What to do

Check your refrigerator and freezer for Kayem, al fresco, Genoa or Triple M products made between March 31 and October 7, 2026. Look for EST. M/P 7839, EST. P 20935A or EST. M 9520 in the USDA mark, then compare the UPC, lot number and expiration date with the FSIS list. Do not eat recalled items: throw them away or return them to the place of purchase. Questions: Kayem customer service, 800-426-6100 or info@kayem.com. If someone develops symptoms after eating one of these products, contact a healthcare provider.
